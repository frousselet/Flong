# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Xcode project targeting iOS, iPadOS and macOS 26.0, from the multiplatform SwiftUI template.
- String catalog with English as the source language and French translations.
- `swift-format` configuration and a shared `Flong` scheme so builds and formatting are reproducible outside Xcode.
- Issue and pull request templates.
- Product and technical specification in `docs/specification.md`, the reference for every design decision.
- Project conventions in `CLAUDE.md`, and the Google Reader API reference kept in `docs/technical/` for the FreshRSS import.
- MPL-2.0 license, with the source notice carried by every file.
- SQLite storage through GRDB, holding the local data model of the specification behind versioned migrations, with UUIDv7 identifiers that sort by creation time.
- Subscriptions : following a feed by its canonical URL, grouped by the publisher serving it under a name the reader may write over each group, which is also the name and the favicon every article wears wherever it is shown, with renaming, making a source a favourite, unsubscribing, and a batch path that lands a whole import in one transaction.
- OPML import, tolerant of the malformed files exporters produce, walking the nesting for every feed at the bottom of it and reporting the lines it could not use, with a screen listing the subscriptions it brought over.
- Private and authenticated feeds : a per-subscriber secret address, HTTP Basic, a bearer token or a fixed header, kept in the keychain and propagated by iCloud Keychain, never in the database, a log, an error message or an export.
- Sessions on sites the reader subscribes to : signing in happens from the article's own menu, on the site's own page in a web view, only the session it leaves is kept, one sign-in covers every feed of the site, it is sent to that site and no other, and a session the site has ended says so rather than quietly serving teasers.
- Full-text extraction : an article opens on whichever version the reader last chose, carried between their devices by the iCloud key-value store, and asking for the whole one fetches the page behind it once, pulls the article out of the navigation, sidebars, share bars and comments around it, and keeps it beside the feed's version.
- HTML parsing and whitelist sanitization, written without a dependency, dropping scripts, frames, forms and tracking pixels, and resolving every address against the article before vetting its scheme, raising to TLS the ones the view fetches itself since App Transport Security refuses a plain one outright, and leaving a link exactly as the publisher wrote it since the browser opens that one and has its own opinion about upgrading.
- Feed parsing for RSS 0.9x, 1.0 and 2.0, Atom 1.0, JSON Feed 1.1 and h-feed, with feed discovery from a page, tolerance for the malformed feeds publishers serve, and a corpus of them under test.
- Conditional fetching with a token bucket per host, `Retry-After` honoured, backoff with jitter, a body cap enforced while streaming, and a refresh interval derived from each feed's own publication history, a background pass spending its short budget on whichever feeds are most overdue against their own rhythm rather than on whichever were subscribed to first, standing aside in Low Power Mode, never over a network the reader pays for by the megabyte, and never counting against a publisher a request the system would not send at all, which used to quarantine every feed a reader had after two days on a tethered connection.
- Ingestion of fetched feeds into the store, recognizing articles already seen, and the same article arriving through two feeds of one newsroom, which is kept but shown once, refreshing them without touching read or starred state, tracking feed health and quarantining what stays broken, with retention by age and by volume, keeping the address of the picture that stands for each article, stated by the feed or taken from its body, reading the two date spellings that were silently losing an article's own date and leaving it sorted on the day it arrived, saying so when an article carries no date of its own rather than passing off its arrival as a publication, and saying when a publisher went back to an article, after its publication date and never instead of it, since an update is information about an article and not a new place for it in a stream ordered by when things were written.
- The reading interface, on iPhone, iPad and Mac : one column at a time under a system tab bar of four sections, the sources, the subjects and the notices each opened as a panel over the page from a button in the leading corner of every section rather than from a tab or a menu of their own, untitled since the button that opened them names them, and closed by a flick rather than by a button repeating what the gesture offers, a sidebar on Mac, each section keeping its own stack, a stream of everything as it arrives broken by day beside the digest, under a pinned row of bars counting a day an hour at a time, exactly the hours it has, every hour with nothing in it greyed to the height of the shortest bar whether it is past or still to come, running the full width of the column until the list moves under it and narrowing into a glass capsule as it does, following the hour the reader has scrolled to rather than being dragged, carrying no count of what is unread, a read article marked by a tick at the end of its headline rather than dimmed, with an isolated web view following the system appearance, reading and starring, marking a view read, adding a feed from the address of a site, and a page that keeps itself up to date : the window follows the store, so a change from a background refresh, from another device or from an archive reaches it without anyone asking, a burst of them settling into one reload and the article list left alone while an article is open, every automatic trigger going through one entry point that fetches, groups and reads the page back so the front page - Full-text index over every stored article, kept in step by triggers, insensitive to accents and to endings, rebuildable at any time, with the language of an article detected on the device as it arrives.
- Query language with fields, states, dates, brackets and exclusion, parsed into a tree and compiled to SQL with every value bound, understanding the FreshRSS syntax, and ranking results by relevance when the index can answer the whole query.
- Search from the article list, following what is typed, completing feed and tag names, and held to the targets of the specification by a test over 125,000 articles.
- Marks and collections : starring, writing on or filing an article writes a mark on the article itself, with no second copy anywhere, which is synchronized and which no purge may take, shown as a grid of squares of three natures : the ones every reader has, which are the articles they starred, the ones they wrote on and everything a favourite source has served, the ones they fill article by article, and the ones they describe once and never fill, each carrying between devices only what it is made of, and every marked article handed to Spotlight and found in the system search.
- Synchronization between a reader's devices through their own private CloudKit database, told of another device's changes by a silent push, carrying subscriptions with what the reader singled out and the names they wrote over their publishers, one record per marked article whose deletion is what carries an unmarking, and read states compacted into one record per month, keeping what the server says about each record so a second save is not refused, carrying the whole stream, its recent days as one record per feed and per day rather than one per article and the rest as append-only files in iCloud Documents, one per device and per day, so that no two devices ever write one file, with nothing purged unless the reader asks for their disk back, and iCloud's refusals surfaced rather than retried blindly, with a development command that forgets the change tokens, the tags the server gave each record, the archives already read and everything the model wrote, so that a repair fetches the whole zone back down instead of only sending everything up and writes the headlines and the subjects again rather than finding nothing left to do.
- Long work done in resumable batches and in the background : the feeds an import left unfetched, and a vector for every marked article, computed on the device and shared between them, which lets what the reader kept be searched by meaning rather than by words.
- A full pass when the device is at rest on the mains, shaped after the one Photos runs : every feed rather than those politeness says are due, then the enrichment, the purge, the index and the exchange with iCloud, on a six-hour interval with a floor between two runs and enough jitter that a reader's devices do not wake together to ask the same publishers at the same second, one refresh at a time whichever of the six triggers asked for it, one the reader asked for waiting its turn rather than being refused where they cannot see it, taken by the outermost entry point so that the nightly pass, the clock and the reader's own command can never ask three hundred publishers the same question at the same second, its clock kept on disk and counted from the last pass rather than from whenever something asked, so that it is not pushed out of reach by every launch and every return to the foreground, a pass that runs out of time reported as the ordinary success it is rather than as a failure the scheduler answers by granting less time, and a task that finds no window doing the work against a model of its own rather than silently doing nothing.
- A digest as the main screen : articles grouped into stories, the ones several rooms are covering right now shown as they arrive, a room being a newsroom rather than a feed so a paper with a feed per desk counts once, named and summarized on the device by the system model in the reader's own language whatever language the articles are in and with no date in what it writes since it is shown none, to the rules a desk would apply : every word carrying information, a named actor and a verb of action before a general idea, the words a reader looks for first since a headline is read in a list and often cut short, clarity before cleverness, a ceiling of twelve words that is enforced rather than asked for, never a promise the articles do not keep, and a line beneath that states the angle and answers what the headline had no room for, of who, what, where and why but never when, in one or two sentences under an enforced ceiling, instead of saying the headline again, each one stating the rooms, the count and the shape of the arrival it rests on, and crediting the picture it carries to the publisher whose article it came with, their name in the corner of it on a pill of glass, drawn smaller on a thumbnail than on the lead so it takes the same share of either, and never a byline since what reaches a feed is where a picture came from and never who made it.
- Subjects across the page, filed by the system model and shown as pills pinned at the head of it and as a rubric above each headline : a catalogue of fifty standard sections seeded at the first launch, in the reader's own language and named in English in the code, which a story is filed into once, one story per call and the answer chosen from the catalogue itself with no way out of choosing, the model writing none of its own since what came of letting it was a drift of near synonyms of the sections that already existed, told apart from a model that could not be asked at all so a moment's failure does not leave a story unfiled for good, an empty vocabulary counting as a question never put rather than as an answer, a section that is renamed taking its stories and the reader's opinion with it, asked with the permissive content-transformation guardrails since the default ones refuse a great deal of ordinary news, greedily so a rebuild does not rewrite a page being read, and which the reader may add their own subjects to.
- A long press on a subject says more of this or less of this, on a score from minus three to three that orders the stories before their weight does, and orders the pills themselves ; a panel lists every subject there is, in the reader's own alphabet, including the ones that have fallen off the page, since a preference nobody can find is a preference nobody can undo.
- A reader's menu in the same corner of every section, wearing the reader's own face : an optional name and picture kept in their own iCloud with nowhere to be sent, and the sites they are signed in to, opened as a panel of its own like the three in the other corner ; the subjects and the notices sit beside the sources instead, since what a reader says in either is said about the page behind it, and the command that asked for a refresh by hand is gone with the menu it was a line in, the page keeping itself up to date through the clock, the full pass at rest and the watcher that follows the store.
- The language a summary was asked in travels with it, so changing the language of a device rewrites the page rather than leaving it in a language its reader no longer reads, a summary that comes back in the wrong language is dropped for the article's own headline, and a command at the foot of the sources list asks the model to write it all again.
- The sources list says when there is no model on this device, and which of the three reasons it is, so a page named after its own articles is not mistaken for a broken one.
- The digest is titled with the day's date rather than with its own name, and orders what the reader has said nothing about by when it happened rather than by how many articles it has gathered, so a story that has run all week stops holding the top of a page that has changed overnight.
- An editorial page rather than a control panel : one readable column, serif for what was written and sans for what the application says about it, hairline rules in place of cards, and Liquid Glass left to the system's own navigation bar, where Apple's guidance keeps it.
- Pictures on the page : the first story runs its picture across the column and the others keep one beside them, which one leads being read from the same page the rows are built from so a refresh cannot leave the page with no lead at all, all at three by two, edged with a hairline inside their own corner so a picture that ends in white still stops somewhere, set beside the summary rather than beside the whole story, so a headline keeps the full width of the page above them, decoded at the size they are drawn and cached on disk rather than fetched twice.
- The mark of each publisher beside its name, on every article row, at the head of an article, once at the head of its group in the sources list and in place of the count of rooms on a story, asked for once per publisher rather than once per feed, cropped round with the same hairline inside them so a column of them reads as one column, taken from what one of its feeds states or from the well-known paths a site keeps one at, falling back to a generic mark rather than a hole, and a download cut short asked for again rather than held against the address for the life of the process.
- Notifications, switched on one by one in the reader's own menu and off until they are : the first tells them when a story opens, leading with its own headline, written by the device that shows it since there is no server to send one, asked for at the moment the reader asks for it rather than at first launch, silent while they are looking at the page it would be about, opening that story when it is tapped, and asking of a story exactly what the front page asks of it so that nobody is told about news they then cannot find anywhere.
